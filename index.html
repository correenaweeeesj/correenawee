    • Mentoring

    • Service under pressure

    What I took forward

    It turns out a morning rush is a decent field study. You see the same people, the same pressures and the same small decisions, every day, with immediate feedback. (Usually about the milk.)

  3. WAVED, a beverage technology start-up · Apr 2023 – Jan 2026

    Operations Manager

    This is where I stopped asking people to try harder and started fixing whatever made the job hard. A good system encourages good behaviour, then rewards it.

    • Service design
    • Learning playbooks
    • Applied psychology
    • Science communication
    Key result
    Cut onboarding from 4 weeks to 2 and improved service consistency by 40% with standardised learning playbooks.
    What I took forward
    How to build systems that reward good behaviour.
    Dig deeperBack to the surface

    There was a lot of science behind how our technology enhanced the taste of a drink, and plenty of customers just wanted a good coffee. So I straddled the line between tech and artistry, and translated.

    Removing the jargon and retraining staff to read each customer lifted engagement and satisfaction scores by 35%.

    When people skip a step, the step is usually the problem. That idea came straight from the café, and it’s still the first thing I check.

Which brings us to behavioural analysis

It’s the first job title I’ve seen that asks for all of it at once: the why, the pressure, the system and the follow-through. I’m not swapping careers. I’m finally using the whole thing.

I’m also teaching myself SQL and starting an introduction to Python at 42 Singapore, because the why is easier to find when you can ask the data yourself.

Two ways to explain one coffee

Problem
WAVED’s drinks had serious science behind them. But when we explained the technology or its benefits, customers either zoned out or stayed sceptical.
Outcome
Customer engagement and satisfaction scores rose by 35%.
  • Applied psychology
  • Science communication
  • Staff training
How I got thereFold it up

I reframed how we talked about the science and took out the jargon. Then I trained staff in two trains of thought: one for customers who just wanted their coffee, and one for those curious about how the technology worked.

Staff also learnt to spot the cues for which customer was which, while keeping the service excellent either way.

One faculty circle, fewer silos

Outcome
One unified structure, with those gaps closed in line with the guidelines.
  • Systems
  • Stakeholders
  • Compliance
How I got thereFold it up

baristamap: making calibration less intimidating

Problem
Most of what it takes to dial in a good coffee lives in a barista’s hands, and “you’ll know it when you taste it” isn’t much of a lesson. Existing tools log the numbers, but few help people understand them.
Outcome
A solo UX project, finished in July 2025: a tested prototype that guides a daily brew, with resources in one place. Try the prototype
  • User research
  • Usability testing
  • Coffee knowledge
How I got thereFold it up

I compared three existing tools and interviewed 15 people: 8 professional baristas and 7 home baristas. Then I built a persona and user flow, and went from wireframes to a prototype. Usability tests led to a clearer graph and consistent navigation buttons.

What I took away: people rarely lack data. They lack a way to make sense of it. The best help looks less like a spreadsheet and more like a patient coach.
